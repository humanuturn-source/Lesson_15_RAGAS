SECTION 1: THE ORIGIN OF LIQUID WATER AND ARCHAEAN SEAS
================================================================================
1.1 VOLATILE DEGASSING PERIOD (4.4 - 4.0 Billion Years Ago)
As Earth's molten surface cooled, its early atmosphere was saturated with water vapor, carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and ammonia. 
* Condensation Trigger: Once surface temperatures dropped below 100°C at local atmospheric pressure, torrential rains began. This continuous downpour lasted for millions of years, filling low-lying basalt basins to create the first oceans.
* Mineral Accumulation: Early rivers eroded acidic surface rocks, carrying dissolved ions (sodium, iron, magnesium, chloride) into these early basins, setting the baseline salinity levels for the rest of Earth's history.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: SUPERCONTINENT RIFT SEQUENCES AND ANCIENT OCEANS
================================================================================
Ocean basins are temporary geographical features that open and close through the 300-to-500-million-year cycle of plate tectonics, known as the Wilson Cycle.

2.1 THE PANTHALASSIC OCEAN (Late Paleozoic to Early Mesozoic Era)
* Temporal Window: 300 Million to 175 Million Years Ago.
* Geographical Scale: A massive global ocean that surrounded the supercontinent Pangea. It covered nearly 70% of Earth's surface area.
* Modern Descendant: The Pacific Ocean formed directly from the closing stages of the Panthalassic basin as Pangea broke apart.

2.2 THE TETHYS OCEAN (Mesozoic Era)
* Spatial Context: A wedge-shaped tropical ocean situated between the ancient continents of Laurasia and Gondwana.
* Modern Descendants: The Mediterranean Sea, Black Sea, and Caspian Sea are remaining pockets of this ancient tropical waterway.
* Economic Value: Massive accumulation of marine organic matter along the shallow Tethyan shelves eventually formed today's primary Middle Eastern oil and gas fields.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: PALEOCEANOGRAPHIC ANXIOUS EVENTS (OAEs)
================================================================================
During specific warm historical periods, global ocean circulation stopped moving vertical water masses, triggering Ocean Anoxic Events (OAEs).
* Mechanisms: Rapid volcanic activity released massive amounts of CO2, warming polar surface waters. Without cold polar waters to sink and carry oxygen down, the deep ocean lost its oxygen supply.
* Geological Fingerprint: Thick layers of organic-rich black shales formed on the seafloor, trapping huge amounts of organic carbon.
