SECTION 1: THE BLUE ECONOMY AND INTERCONTINENTAL LOGISTICS
================================================================================
Human civilization depends on the ocean for international trade, energy resources, and global food security.

1.1 MARITIME TRADE CORRIDORS AND STRATEGIC CHOKEPOINTS
Over 80% of global trade by volume is carried by sea lanes. Global shipping traffic relies on key geographic chokepoints:
* The Strait of Malacca: Connects the Indian Ocean to the South China Sea. Over 90,000 transport vessels pass through annually, carrying oil and manufactured goods across Asia.
* The Strait of Hormuz: The world's most critical oil chokepoint, linking the Persian Gulf to the open Arabian Sea.
* The Suez Canal: An artificial shortcut crossing Egypt to connect the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, eliminating the need to sail all the way around Africa.

1.2 AQUACULTURE AND MARINE PROTEIN DEPLETION
* Catch Metrics: Global wild capture fisheries extract approximately 90 million metric tons of marine biomass every year.
* Overfishing Impact: Industrial fishing networks have pushed more than 34% of global wild fish stocks past their sustainable biological recovery limits.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: CLIMATE INTERACTION AND CHEMICAL CHANGES
================================================================================
The ocean absorbs the majority of excess heat and greenhouse gases generated by human industrial activity, which is changing its fundamental chemistry.

2.1 OCEAN ACIDIFICATION MATRIX [REF: CHEM-ACID-01]
The absorption of carbon dioxide (CO2) into surface waters triggers a direct chemical shift that reduces available carbonate ions.

CHEMICAL PATHWAY FLOW SYSTEM:
Industrial CO2 Emission -> Ocean Surface Absorption -> Formation of Carbonic Acid (H2CO3) -> Dissociation into Hydrogen Ions (H+) and Bicarbonate (HCO3-) -> Decrease in Ambient Seawater pH -> Dissolution of Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3) Shells.

2.2 OCEAN THERMAL STRESS AND EXPANSION
The ocean has absorbed over 90% of the excess heat trapped in Earth's atmosphere from greenhouse gas emissions.
* Thermal Expansion: As water warms, it expands, causing roughly one-third of global sea-level rise.
* Marine Heatwaves (MHWs): Periods of extreme water temperatures that trigger mass coral bleaching events, breaking down the symbiotic relationship between corals and their photosynthetic zooxanthellae algae.
